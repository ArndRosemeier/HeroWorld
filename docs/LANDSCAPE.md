# The landscape

The plan lives in the [HeroWorld landscape plan](https://claude.ai/artifact/2EUBMoi59Qj9ZKbGZmXYxC); this file says what is built.

## Layers

1. **Globe** (`hw-gen::planet::globe`). The planet's shape as one analytic function of a point on the unit sphere: `Globe::elevation(unit, finest_m)` is the height above sea level in metres, with detail down to about `finest_m`. Sixteen tectonic plates (four in ten continental) give continents, ocean floors, mountain belts where plates close, island arcs and trenches at sea, rifts where they part. Seamless 3D noise (`hw_core::Noise3`) breaks them into several landmasses with ragged coasts, adds hills, wandering mountain chains and worn inland ranges. Sea level is calibrated per seed so 30% of the surface is land. Finer detail only adds octaves; the coarse shape does not change.
2. **Grid** (`planet::grid`). A cube-sphere of `n x n` cells per face, equiangular, so cells are nearly equal and there are no poles. `n = 512` gives cells of about 20 km. `par_map` runs a function over all cells on every core, in order.
3. **PlanetMap** (`planet::map`). What needs the whole planet, solved once on the grid:
   - Temperature: warm at the equator, cold at the poles, 6.5 °C a km up.
   - Rain: moist air carried by the trade winds, westerlies and polar easterlies, picked up over the sea and dropped over rising ground, so windward coasts and mountains are wet and the far side dry. Latitude bands make the equatorial and sub-polar wet belts and the dry subtropics.
   - Rivers and lakes: Priority-Flood fills every basin (so every river reaches the sea), the rain runs off and adds up downhill in m³/s. 60 m³/s makes a river on the map; a basin filled more than 6 m is a lake.
   - Biomes (`planet::biome`): ice, tundra, taiga, steppe, grassland, shrubland, temperate forest, desert, savanna, seasonal forest, rainforest, alpine, plus ocean and lake. `Biome::tree_cover` says how wooded each is.
4. **Drama** (Arnd 2026-11-14: the planet should be more dramatic than a real one, light supernatural common, special things rare). `Globe::elevation` has tall ranges (to 12 km), mesa country (item 8) and deep canyons. `Globe::aether(unit)` is the Aether field, 0 to 1: most land sits around 0.2 to 0.5 and thin ley lines climb towards 1. `Globe::solid(p, above_ground)` is the truly 3D part (floating rock in strong Aether, caves and overhangs in rocky ground), a density to add to the ground, meant for Engine's `WorldGenerator::density`. `planet::wonders` places 16 named wonders (two of each of 8 kinds, 1700 km apart) where the Aether is strongest and the land suits them. The start region is in a temperate zone and stays close to normal.
5. **Home and ground in the game** (`planet::home`, 0.306). `choose_home` picks a temperate river mouth with weak Aether and mountains within 50 km. `PlanetPatch` is the planet's ground in a place's local metres (x east, z south): `ground(p)` is the height above the sea (flat, without the planet's curve: the sea is drawn flat to the horizon, and curved ground sank low country 20 km off under it), `site` says what the place is like (site kind, relief, river width and direction, which way and how far the sea lies). The patch stands where the real coast is about 700 m off. `TownParams::on_planet` takes those as the town's setting (`seaward`, `coast_distance`, `flow` override the town's own picks). `TownWorld::generate_on_or_stop` blends the town's own land into the planet's ground from 72% of the planned land's half-width outward, and `TownWorld::ground` is what the client, the server and collision all read. `GeneratedTown::generate_on_planet*` is what the game uses; `generate` (flat, seed-made) stays for tests. `planet_map` also writes `-home.png`: 60 km around the home with the town's box in red.
   Since 0.307 the town's land is the planet's: `TownParams::planet` hands the patch to `Land::generate`, which marks every cell below sea level as sea (the real, ragged coast; `Land::sea` only says whether there is one) and takes the planet's heights for its hills, with banks falling to the water no steeper than 1 in 3. `urban::outer::OuterRivers` carries each river on upstream of the town along the lowest ground (to 25 km, or to where the land stands 150 m high), narrowing to a brook, and `TownWorld::ground` cuts its valley into the planet's ground. `choose_home` and the patch look for open sea (still under water 1.5 and 3 km out), not a pond.
6. **Country beyond the town** (0.316). `PlanetPatch` carries the planet's climate on a 16 km grid; `PlanetPatch::cover(p, height)` gives a point's `Cover`: how green the ground is, the snow line (where the yearly mean falls under -1 °C), how wooded it is (the biome's `tree_cover`, thinning to nothing at the tree line) and the temperature. The client hands green and snow line to Engine (`WorldGenerator::ground_cover`, bare ground is scree), near and far alike, and `urban::greenery::wild_trees` plants the country's woods from the same cover (pines where it is cold). `Globe::elevation` has a highlands term: rugged country of crags some way inland, so a home is never all gentle hills, and `choose_home` scores the peaks 15 and 30 km off. The view reaches 64 km (`settings::VIEW_DISTANCE`). `HW_VIEW=mountains` and `HW_VIEW=woods` look at it.
   Known limits: trees stand only within the content distance (1.2 km) of the hero; distant woods are ground colour only.
7. **Rivers that climb** (Engine 0.071). Beyond the town a river is a flight of pools (`urban::outer::OuterRivers::levels`, one level per point of the centre line): each pool is level, and at its lower end the water runs over a sill and falls to the next. A new pool starts once the land has risen 1.5 to 4 m over the last one; a single fall is at most 18 m, so steep ground gives a cascade in a gorge (to 150 m deep), gentle ground long pools. Almost half of the pools four points or longer widen into a lake (120 to 500 m wider in the middle). The river's head is where the land stops rising (the divide), 25 km out at most; a spring there (`OuterRivers::springs`) feeds it. Falls are horseshoes with a ragged lip; a brook pours through a 12 m notch, a broad river over 45% of its width. `OuterRivers::carve` cuts bed, sill and banks (a levee where the land lies lower than the water), `TownWorld::water_level(p)` is the level at a point (0 for the sea and the town's own water). The client hands levels and springs to Engine (`WorldGenerator::water_level`, `water_sources`): near the hero the water is simulated and really falls, far off it is drawn as sheets. `HW_VIEW=falls|cascade|headwater` look at it; `cargo run --release -p hw-gen --example river_profile -- <seed>` prints a river pool by pool.
   Known limits: inside the town all water still stands at sea level, so a town on high ground has its river in a cut and the first falls right at its edge. The planet's own rivers (from `PlanetMap`) are not yet the ones drawn, and there are no lakes away from a river. Heroes do not swim yet.

8. **Mesa country** (0.318; Arnd 2026-11-14: mesas in stacked tiers round the city). `Globe::mesa(unit)` says how much a place is mesa country (a broad field, plus the home's own: `Globe::set_home_mesas` makes everything within 70 km of the home mesa country, fading out to 126 km, on the globe a `PlanetPatch` reads; the planet map keeps the planet as made). There, noise a few km across is cut into up to five tiers: level tables at about 77, 198, 363, 572 and 825 m above the sea, each with a sheer cliff down to the one below (a cliff takes 7% of a tier's width). Land that stands higher than a tier keeps its own height, and ground under 4 m stays a shore. `Globe::elevation_parts` gives the valley floor (`PlanetPatch::lowland`) and the full ground. A town is planned on the floor; the tables rise from its edge outwards, to full height about 800 m on (160 to 1440 m by side, `urban::world::mesa_share`). Rivers run on the floor too, and a table lying across one is cut back from the water: flat for 45 m, the walls at full height 190 m off (`OuterRivers::canyon`), so the river runs through it in a canyon. `PlanetPatch::new` starts at the river's mouth (first shore on the way from the home cell to the sea cell its river runs into), then picks the shore with the best country behind it (`country`: land rather than an islet, tables in sight) and backs 700 m off the nearest water; `choose_home` skips islets and lagoons. `HW_VIEW=mesas` and `HW_VIEW=canyon` look at it.
9. Next: wonders and the Aether's look in the game, `Globe::solid` as real 3D rock, settlements and roads. See the plan.

Solving a planet at `n = 512` takes about 3 s on all cores; do it on a worker thread and cache it by seed and `WORLD_VERSION`.

## Looking at it

```
cargo run --release -p hw-gen --example planet_map -- <seed> screenshots/planet [n]
```

Writes `-world.png` (biomes, shading, rivers), `-rain.png`, `-height.png`, three globe views `-globe0..2.png`, and prints landmasses, rain and height percentiles and the share of each biome. Open the PNGs and look: this is how the climate was tuned.

## Tests

`planet::globe` (same seed same planet, land share, no seams at poles or date line, finer detail keeps the coarse shape), `planet::grid` (cells round-trip, neighbours across face edges), `planet::map` (deterministic, warm equator, every river reaches the sea, rain varies).
